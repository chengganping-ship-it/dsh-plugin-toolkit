# Job Application Tracker
*Columns + categories to track every application. Copy into Google Sheets / Excel / Notion. Keep it simple — a tracker you actually use beats a perfect one you ignore.*

---

## Columns (set these as headers)

| Date | Company | Role | Source | Job URL | Status | Applied Date | Follow-up Due | Contact Name | Contact Email | Notes / Next Step |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

---

## Status values (use a dropdown)

- 🟡 Applied
- 🟢 Interviewing
- 🟣 Final Round
- ✅ Offer
- 🔴 Rejected
- ⚪ No Response (14+ days)

---

## Why this matters

- Your memory is unreliable. A tracker removes the anxiety of "did I apply there?"
- It shows you which SOURCE converts best (LinkedIn vs. company site vs. referral) — double down on winners.
- It creates a natural follow-up schedule, which is where most candidates lose opportunities.

---

## Follow-up cadence (the simple version)

- **Day 3 after applying:** if no response, send a polite 2-line check-in email (see prompt below).
- **Day 7:** if still nothing, move to "No Response" and stop chasing — apply elsewhere.
- **After an interview:** send a thank-you note within 24 hours, and follow up once after 5-7 days if you haven't heard back.

---

## Follow-up email prompt

```
Write a short, polite follow-up email (under 80 words) to a hiring manager / recruiter.

Context:
- I applied for [role] at [company] on [date]
- [Optional: I had an interview on date]
- I want to check on the status without being pushy

Tone: warm, professional, confident. No guilt-tripping, no "just checking in" cliché.
Include one line reaffirming my interest and a specific thing I liked about the role/company.
```

---

## Pro tip

Track at least the "Source" column honestly for 30 days. You'll be surprised which 20% of channels produce 80% of your interviews. Kill the rest and save hours.
