# We built a data archive because evidence is vanishing

If you try to recall the exact GPT-5 pricing at launch in March 2026, you'll hit a wall.

OpenAI's pricing page has been revised 7+ times. Search engines serve the current version. News articles from the period may reflect journalist interpretation, not primary text.

**This is the problem we're solving.**

---

## What's dying

Not "macro trends." Not "AI will change everything" — those are unfalsifiable. We're talking about:

**Specific numbers. Specific wording. Specific dates.**

- GPT-5 standard input price on 2025-08-08 16:30 Beijing: $1.25/M tokens. By September it may be $1.00. By December, nobody will remember.
- The US Commerce Bureau's "Huawei Ascend use is violation" guidance dropped on a Friday afternoon in May 2025. By Monday, CBP's site hosted three competing media interpretations, none quoting original text verbatim.
- The De Minimis termination — $800 Chinese parcels no longer duty-free — went through 2 delays and 1 partial implementation. The Federal Register's precise 2025-08-02 language is now buried under simplified narratives.

These numbers won't appear in research papers — they're "too journalistic." But they'll become irreproducible in 6 months, because nobody archives screenshots of GPT-5 pricing pages.

We do.

---

## Our approach: snapshot, not tracking

This archive doesn't chase news. We release quarterly "snapshots" — documents claiming **"here is what the world looked like on this date."**

Each snapshot contains:

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| Primary prices & policy text | From Tier-1 sources, URL + timestamp |
| Structured data points | YAML, not PDF — machine-parseable |
| Failure case history | Structural precedents of how similar judgments failed |
| "Where we may be wrong" | Mandatory counterargument section |
| Replication path | Steps if you don't trust us — go verify |

Each snapshot is eternally dated (`snapshot/2025-08-08/`). It will never be "updated" — only supplemented or rebutted.

**Old snapshots are never altered.** That's the point.

## Issue one: triple crisis overlay

We chose August 8, 2025 for issue one because three independent data points hit threshold simultaneously:

1. **Inference cost collapse**: GPT-5 vs DeepSeek price gap hits 214x. AWS Q2 margin compressed to 32.9%. Not a trend — today's specific number.
2. **Physical-side supply crisis**: US transformer lead times hit 128 weeks (2.5 years). Not a forecast — Wood Mackenzie's recorded fact today.
3. **Compliance market reshaping**: Section 231 terminates. 3M parcels/day shift from duty-free to mandatory formal entry.

Three events converged in one August. Six months from now, narrative machinery will separate them into "AI crisis," "energy crisis," "trade war" — independent stories.

We record how they looked simultaneous.

## The long game

Long-term value of this archive is not in any single issue.

Imagine you're a regulatory historian in 2030, looking back at 2025-2026. You may need to answer:

- "What was the exact date EU AI Act high-risk enforcement began?"
- "What did De Minimis termination look like in its first quarter?"
- "When did transformer lead times peak?"

Google gives you the latest. Paid databases may retain those figures (if you subscribe). But **timestamped snapshots marked "we visited the URL and content matched"** will be increasingly scarce.

We are that increasingly scarce.
